The Department of Public Utilities (“Department”) reissues this Notice of Filing and 
Request for Comments and extends the deadlines for intervention and comments upon 
learning that the original notice was not served in accordance with the June 25, 2026 Order 
of Notice. 
On June 15, 2026, Massachusetts Electric Company and Nantucket Electric Company 
d/b/a National Grid (“National Grid” or “Company”) filed with the Department a petition for 
approval of revised heat pump rates that incorporate winter transmission charges along with 
related tariff revisions for effect November 1, 2026.  This filing was made pursuant to Seasonal 
Heat Pump Rates, D.P.U. 25-08-A at 21 (June 1, 2026).  The Department has docketed this 
petition as D.P.U. 26-71. 
In 2024, the Department approved seasonal heat pump rates for National Grid as part of 
its base distribution rate proceeding in Massachusetts Electric Company and Nantucket Electric 
Company, D.P.U. 23-150, at 509-513 (2024), which included a revenue-neutral redesign of base 
distribution rate charges but did not incorporate changes to winter transmission charges.  
Subsequently, in D.P.U. 25-08-A at 21, the Department directed the Company to petition the 
Department by June 15, 2026, for approval of proposed winter transmission charges to 
incorporate into its heat pump rates for the 2026-2027 heating season, consistent with the design 
of winter transmission charges approved for NSTAR Electric Company d/b/a Eversource Energy 
in NSTAR Electric Company, D.P.U. 25-55, at 7-8 (2025), along with revised heat pump rate 
tariffs, bill impact analyses, and supporting exhibits, including rate design worksheets. 
In this proceeding, National Grid proposes for effect November 1, 2026, a 
revenue-neutral redesign of winter transmission charges for incorporation into its heat pump 
rates as well as proposed revisions to its R-1 and R-2 retail delivery service tariffs for 
Massachusetts Electric Company, M.D.P.U. No. 1641 (proposed) and M.D.P.U. No. 1642 
(proposed), respectively; its R-1 and R-2 retail delivery service tariffs for Nantucket Electric 
Company, M.D.P.U. No. 709 (proposed) and M.D.P.U. No. 710 (proposed), respectively; and its 
transmission service cost adjustment provision, M.D.P.U. No. 1640 (proposed).

According to the Company, approval of the proposed heat pump rates would result in a 
transmission service charge of $0.00477 per kilowatt-hour (“kWh”) for the period March 2026 
through February 2027.  National Grid states that this charge is based on the final four months of 
the winter season for the current recovery period (i.e., November 2026 through February 2027) 
and is designed to be revenue neutral based on the calendar year transmission costs approved in 
Massachusetts Electric Company and Nantucket Electric Company, D.P.U. 26-02, at 4 
(February 27, 2026).  The Company estimates that the proposed rates would provide heat pump 
customers with monthly bill savings of approximately nine percent relative to standard 
transmission rates.  Beginning March 1, 2027, National Grid proposes to calculate the 
transmission service charge using a six-month winter season and a six-month summer season.  
Based on the proposed normalized transmission service charge of $0.01998 per kWh and the 
heat pump base distribution rate proposed by the Company in Massachusetts Electric Company 
and Nantucket Electric Company, D.P.U. 26-84, National Grid estimates that heat pump 
customers would experience monthly bill savings of approximately 20 percent compared to 
customers taking service under the otherwise applicable rates.  Customers seeking additional 
information regarding the bill impacts of the proposed heat pump rates may contact the Company 
directly. 
Any person interested in commenting on this matter may submit written comments no 
later than the close of business (5:00 p.m.) on Tuesday, July 28, 2026.  Written comments from 
the public may be sent by email to dpu.efiling@mass.gov, Scott.Seigal@mass.gov, and the 
Company’s attorney, Melissa Liazos, Esq., at Melissa.Liazos@nationalgrid.com.  Please note 
that, in the interest of transparency, any comments will be posted to our website as received and 
without redacting personal information, such as addresses, telephone numbers, or email 
addresses.  As such, consider the extent of information you wish to share when submitting 
comments.  The Department strongly encourages public comments to be submitted by email.  If, 
however, a member of the public is unable to send written comments by email, a paper copy may 
be sent to Mark D. Marini, Secretary, Department of Public Utilities, One South Station, Boston, 
Massachusetts, 02110. 
Any person who desires to participate otherwise in the evidentiary phase of this 
proceeding shall file a petition for leave to intervene no later than 5:00 p.m. on Tuesday, 
July 28, 2026.  A petition for leave to intervene must satisfy the timing and substantive 
requirements of 220 CMR 1.03.  Receipt by the Department constitutes filing and determines 
whether a petition has been timely filed.  A petition filed late may be disallowed as untimely, 
unless good cause is shown for waiver under 220 CMR 1.01(4).  To be allowed, a petition under 
220 CMR 1.03(1) must satisfy the standing requirements of G.L. c. 30A, § 10.  All responses to 
petitions to intervene must be filed by the close of business (5:00 p.m.) on the second business 
day after the petition to intervene was filed. 
All documents should be submitted to the Department in .pdf format by e-mail 
attachment to dpu.efiling@mass.gov and Scott.Seigal@mass.gov.  The text of the e-mail must 
specify:  (1) the docket number of the proceeding (D.P.U. 26-71); (2) the name of the person or 
company submitting the filing; and (3) a brief descriptive title of the document.  All documents 
submitted in electronic format will be posted on the Department’s website through our online
